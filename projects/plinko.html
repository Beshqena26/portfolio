physics feel second

An MYBC Originals ball-drop game. The honest secret of every fair plinko: the winning bin is decided by the math before the ball ever moves. The design job is to make a predetermined outcome feel like gravity, and to never lie about which one is in charge.

Choreography, not simulation

A physics simulation that decides outcomes can't be provably fair; a fair outcome with no physics feels like a slot machine wearing a costume. Plinko squares that by drawing the bin from the verifiable math first, then computing a peg-by-peg path that lands the ball exactly there. Every bounce is choreography toward a destination the player can verify afterward.

That inversion drives every visual decision: bounces are springy but never so chaotic that a path looks impossible, the ball accelerates believably, and near-miss bins get a subtle shake when the ball skims past them, tension the math permits, honesty the math requires.

The multiplier wall is the interface

The bins along the bottom are the whole payout table, always on screen. Edge bins carry the big multipliers and glow accordingly; center bins are the frequent, small outcomes. Rapid play is first-class: drops queue without blocking each other, several balls can be in flight at once, and the history strip settles them in the order they land.

Chose: concurrent balls

Players who want volume shouldn't wait out an animation. Each ball carries its own settled outcome; the board is a stage, not a lock.

Rejected: one ball at a time

Simpler state, but it caps the game's rhythm at the animation's length and turns excitement into waiting.
